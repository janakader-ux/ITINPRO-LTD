ITIN vs SSN: Which Number Do You Actually Need?

Should you apply for a Social Security Number or an ITIN, and what happens if you pick the wrong one?

Amira is moving to the U.S. on a work visa next year but will first receive U.S. consulting income from the UK. Her adviser says “ITIN”, her future employer says “SSN”.

The simple rule

If you are eligible to obtain an SSN (for example a U.S. citizen or someone lawfully admitted for employment), you must not apply for an ITIN. An ITIN is only for people who must furnish a taxpayer ID but are not eligible for an SSN.

Visa or work permission? SSN first

If you enter the U.S. with a green card or visa that lets you work, apply for an SSN with the Social Security Administration (SSA) using Form SS-5. Anyone with an Employment Authorization Document (EAD), or already authorised to work, is eligible for an SSN. Only if the SSA rules you ineligible do you complete Form W-7, and the SSA’s letter of denial must be attached or your application will be rejected.

Don’t file while an SSN application is pending

If your SSN application is pending, do not file Form W-7. Wait for the SSA’s decision.

Students and honoraria visitors

F, J and M visa students who will not work, and visitors receiving only honoraria, do not need to go to the SSA first. A letter from the Designated School Official, Responsible Officer or Authorised School Official can be used instead.

Practical takeaway

  • Eligible for an SSN = no ITIN.
  • Work visa or EAD: SSN first; denial letter goes with Form W-7.
  • Non-working students and honoraria visitors can use a school official’s letter.
