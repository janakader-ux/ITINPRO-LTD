Has My ITIN Expired? The Three-Year Rule and the Pre-2013 Rule

You haven’t used your ITIN for a few years. Is it still valid, or will it sink your next return?

Mark’s ITIN was issued in 2015. He filed in 2019 and then nothing until now.

The three-year rule

An ITIN that has not been included on a U.S. federal return at least once in the last three consecutive tax years expires on 31 December of the third consecutive tax year. It must be renewed before it can be used again on a U.S. federal return.

The pre-2013 rule

ITINs assigned before 2013 that were never renewed have expired. Expired ITINs must be renewed to avoid delays in processing your return.

How to tell

  • Look at the year on your ITIN assignment letter.
  • List the tax years your ITIN appeared on a filed U.S. return.
  • Three consecutive years without use means expired; pre-2013 and never renewed also means expired.

What to do

Renew with Form W-7 and your return (unless an exception applies). See our renewal guide.

Practical takeaway

  • Unused for three consecutive tax years = expired on 31 December of the third.
  • Pre-2013 and not renewed = expired.
  • Renew when you need to file again.
