ITIN for a U.S. Bank Account: When Do Banks Require One?

A U.S. bank says it can’t open your account without a U.S. tax ID. Do you need a tax return you haven’t filed yet?

Raj, a UK dentist, wants to hold dollars in a U.S. interest-bearing account for a property purchase. The bank asked for an ITIN.

Interest reported to the IRS

Where an account generates interest subject to IRS information reporting and/or withholding, the bank needs your TIN. That makes “third-party withholding on passive income” – Exception 1 – the usual route.

Exception 1(b): business account

For an interest-bearing account that generates income effectively connected with your U.S. trade or business. Attach an original signed letter from the bank on letterhead, showing your name and stating you have opened a business account subject to IRS reporting and/or withholding on interest during the current tax year.

Exception 1(c): resident alien individual account

For individuals who are resident aliens for tax purposes and have opened an interest-bearing individual account. The bank letter wording is the same, referring to an individual deposit account.

Practical tips

  • Ask the bank for the letter before you apply – a self-written statement is not enough.
  • Write “Exception 1(b)” or “1(c)” on the box h line of Form W-7.
  • Check the letter names you exactly as your passport does.

Practical takeaway

  • Interest subject to IRS reporting = Exception 1(b) or 1(c).
  • The bank’s signed letter on letterhead is the key document.
  • Mismatched names between letter and passport cause delays.

A reader asks

“My bank only gave me an email. Is that enough?”

Usually not. The IRS looks for an original signed letter or document on official letterhead. We can send you a sample letter to give your bank.
