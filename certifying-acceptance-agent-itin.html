Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA): Why Use One for Your ITIN?

Would you post your only passport to an IRS mail-processing centre in Texas and hope it comes back in time for your holiday?

Sam is travelling to Spain in five weeks. The IRS says original documents can be returned within 60 days. He cannot risk being without his passport.

Acceptance agents versus certifying acceptance agents

Acceptance Agents (AAs) are people or firms that have signed formal agreements with the IRS to help applicants obtain ITINs. Certifying Acceptance Agents (CAAs) take on more responsibility: they are authorised to verify your identity and foreign-status documents by reviewing the originals (or certified copies) and confirming they are authentic, accurate and complete.

What a CAA can verify

  • For primary and secondary taxpayers: all documents except foreign military ID cards.
  • For dependants: passports and birth certificates only.
  • The CAA submits the completed Form W-7, any U.S. return, and copies of the documents to the IRS, and receives your ITIN directly from the IRS.

Why that matters to you

Because the CAA reviews your originals, you can keep them. The IRS otherwise returns originals by post, which can take up to 60 days. If you submit school or medical records for a dependant, the original must still be submitted whichever route you use.

Costs

The IRS charges nothing for an ITIN. A person acting as an acceptance agent may charge a fee for their service. Acceptance agents assist with the process; only the IRS issues the ITIN.

Practical takeaway

  • A CAA authenticates your original documents and returns them.
  • Dependants: CAAs verify passports and birth certificates only.
  • The IRS charges no fee; agents may charge for their service.
