ITIN for the Spouse of a U.S. Citizen or Resident: Joint Returns Explained

You’re married to an American and you live in Britain. Can you file jointly with your spouse without a Social Security Number?

Claire and her husband (a U.S. citizen working in London) want to file a joint U.S. return. Claire has no SSN and no right to work in the U.S.

The rule since 2018

Because the deduction for personal exemptions was suspended from tax year 2018, a spouse or dependant is not eligible for an ITIN unless they are claimed for an allowable tax benefit or file their own return. A spouse filing a joint return is one of those benefits.

Applying under box e

Tick box e (spouse of U.S. citizen/resident alien) and enter the U.S. spouse’s full name and SSN or ITIN. The U.S. return must be attached to Form W-7, showing joint filing.

Documents

  • Claire’s original passport (stand-alone proof of identity and foreign status), or a certified copy.
  • The joint U.S. return, attached to Form W-7.
  • Where Claire could work in the U.S. (visa or EAD), an SSA denial letter first.

An older exception to ignore

Spouses claimed as an exemption can only be so for tax years 2017 and earlier. For 2018 onwards it is the joint return (or another allowable tax benefit) that counts.

Practical takeaway

  • Spouses qualify through an allowable tax benefit, such as a joint return.
  • Box e needs your spouse’s name and SSN/ITIN.
  • Attach the joint return to Form W-7.
