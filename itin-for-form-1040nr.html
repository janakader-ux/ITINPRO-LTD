ITIN for Form 1040-NR: When a Non-Resident Alien Must File a U.S. Return

You have never lived in America but U.S. tax was taken from your income. How do you get it back – and why does the IRS want a number you don’t have?

Mei lives in Cambridge and received U.S. royalties with 30% withheld. She is not eligible for an SSN and wants to file to claim a refund.

Box b: non-resident alien filing a U.S. return

A non-resident alien individual not eligible for an SSN who is required to file a U.S. return, or who files only to claim a refund, applies on Form W-7 with the return attached (Form 1040-NR). Under this reason you must give your complete foreign address on line 3.

Documents

  • Original passport (valid, not expired) or certified copy.
  • Your U.S. return attached to Form W-7.
  • A foreign tax ID (for example a UK UTR or National Insurance number) on line 6b if you have one.

A filing purpose is essential

The IRS issues ITINs for a genuine federal tax purpose: a legal filing requirement, a refund claim, or reporting income even if no tax is due. A return that contains all zero entries generally will not show a purpose.

Estimated tax while you wait

If you receive income with no tax withheld you may need to make estimated payments even before you have an ITIN. Write “ITIN TO BE REQUESTED” where the ITIN or SSN is asked for. Do not file Form W-7 with the payment voucher.

Practical takeaway

  • Box b = non-resident alien filing a return; return attached to Form W-7.
  • Always provide your complete foreign address under box b.
  • No purpose, no ITIN: nil returns do not usually qualify.

A reader asks

“I’m not sure if I’m a resident or non-resident for U.S. tax. Does it change the form?”

Yes: it changes the reason box (b or c) and the proof you give. The substantial presence test decides it. Tell us your day-counts and we’ll confirm the correct route.
