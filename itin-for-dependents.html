ITIN for a Dependent Living Abroad or in the U.S.: Who Qualifies?

You’re a U.S. taxpayer in the UK with a child, parent or relative who has no SSN. Can you still claim them – and how?

Marcus is American, lives in Kent, and supports his mother in Mexico. His accountant asked him to “get an ITIN for Mum”.

Dependants need an allowable tax benefit

From tax year 2018, a dependant can only get (or renew) an ITIN if they are claimed for an allowable tax benefit and are listed on your U.S. return with the right schedule, form or checked box. Merely listing them is not enough.

Residency proof

Dependants must prove U.S. residency unless they are dependants of U.S. military personnel stationed overseas, or they are proving foreign status from Canada or Mexico and are claimed for a benefit other than the credit for other dependents (for example head of household). Others can show a passport with a U.S. date of entry, or one of: a U.S. school record, medical record, state ID, driving licence, visa, or (adults) a U.S. bank statement, rental statement or utility bill.

Documents

  • Passport (CAAs can verify passports and birth certificates for dependants).
  • Birth certificate for children under 18 unless a passport is used.
  • A photo ID for dependants aged 14 or over (18 for students).

Practical takeaway

  • No allowable tax benefit = no ITIN for a dependant.
  • Canada/Mexico dependants may be exempt from residency proof.
  • Check the ‘credit for other dependents’ box on the return yourself.

A reader asks

“My son is a U.S. citizen with a passport but no SSN. Does he need an ITIN?”

A U.S. citizen is eligible for an SSN and should not apply for an ITIN. Tell us about your son’s circumstances and we’ll point you to the right process.
