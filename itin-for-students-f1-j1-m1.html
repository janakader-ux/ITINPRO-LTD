ITIN for F-1, J-1 and M-1 Students: Scholarships, Fellowships and the SEVIS Letter

You’ve won a scholarship at a U.S. university. The bursar needs a tax ID – but you can’t work and have no SSN. Now what?

Kai is a UK postgraduate visiting a U.S. university on a J-1 visa with a fellowship.

No SSN trip for non-working students

Students on F-1, J-1 or M-1 visas who will not work do not have to go to the SSA first. A letter from the Designated School Official or Responsible Officer stating you will not take employment or receive income from personal services can be used instead.

What the SEVIS certification letter must contain

  • Original letterhead with a verifiable address.
  • Applicant’s full name and SEVIS number, and confirmation of SEVIS registration.
  • That you presented an unexpired passport, visa or other ID (a U.S. visa is not needed if the foreign address is in Canada or Mexico), with the documents listed.
  • Signed and dated by a PDSO, DSO, RO or ARO, with a verifiable phone number.
  • Attached copies of the identity documents, and Form DS-2019 and/or I-20.

2(b) versus 2(c)

  • 2(b) – treaty benefit claimed: add Form W-8BEN, treaty country and article on Form W-7, and the award letter.
  • 2(c) – no treaty: add a DSO/RO letter that the income is non-compensatory and subject to IRS reporting this year.

Practical takeaway

  • Ask your DSO early – the SEVIS letter is the long pole.
  • 2(b) needs Form W-8BEN; 2(c) needs the DSO income letter.
  • A separate letter is needed for each applicant, including spouse and dependants.
