Acceptable ITIN Documents: The 13 IRS-Approved IDs (and Why Passport Wins)

You have a UK driving licence, a utility bill and a bank statement. Surely that’s plenty for the IRS?

Daniel doesn’t want to part with his passport and thinks his UK driving licence and birth certificate will do.

The only list that matters

The IRS accepts 13 document types: passport (stand-alone); USCIS photo ID; U.S. Department of State visa; U.S. driving licence; U.S. military ID; foreign driving licence; foreign military ID; national ID card (current, with name, address, photo, date of birth and expiry); U.S. state ID; foreign voter’s registration card; civil birth certificate; medical records (dependants under 6); school records (dependants under 24).

Passport = stand-alone

A valid passport (or a certified copy) proves both identity and foreign status. For dependants, a passport without a U.S. date of entry cannot be the only document, except for dependants from Canada or Mexico or military dependants stationed overseas.

No passport? Two documents

Without a passport you must provide a combination of at least two current documents that show name and photograph and support your claim of foreign status. At least one must have a recent photograph, apart from children under 14 (under 18 if students).

Original or certified only

Originals, or copies certified by the issuing agency. Notarised copies are only accepted for dependants and spouses of U.S. military personnel applying from overseas. Copies of documents with information on both sides must show both sides.

Practical takeaway

  • Only 13 document types count.
  • Passport is the only stand-alone one.
  • Keep your originals safe – use a CAA.

A reader asks

“My UK driving licence and birth certificate: enough without a passport?”

Possibly, but a foreign driving licence mostly proves identity, while a civil birth certificate proves foreign status. We’ll check the combination against the table and tell you.
