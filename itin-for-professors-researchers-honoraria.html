ITIN for Visiting Professors, Researchers and Honoraria Payments

A U.S. university wants to pay you £2,000 for a three-day guest lecture. They can’t pay without your tax ID. Do you really need an SSN?

Prof. Ahmed is visiting New York for a conference on a B-1 visa and will be paid an honorarium.

Honoraria-only visitors

People present in the U.S. who receive only honoraria do not need to apply for an SSN or obtain an SSA denial letter. A letter from the Authorised School Official who extended the invitation, explaining the purpose of the visit and that an honorarium will be paid, is enough.

Wages or compensation under a treaty

For wages, salary, or compensation with treaty benefits you submit an employment letter, contract or invitation letter, the information on Form W-7 that you claim a treaty, and a completed Form 8233. Where you could work in the U.S. you also attach an SSA denial letter.

Filling in Form W-7

  • Box a (treaty) and box h with “Exception 2(a) – Honoraria Payment” or “Wages”.
  • Treaty country and article number.
  • Line 6g: the university or company, city and state, and your length of stay (it can be as short as a few days).

Practical takeaway

  • Honoraria-only visitors need the official’s letter, not an SSA letter.
  • Employment ITINs need Form 8233 and a contract or letter.
  • Don’t skip line 6g.

A reader asks

“I’m only visiting for two days. Do I really need to do all this?”

If the university must report or withhold on your payment this year, yes. We’ll review the letter you have and tell you what’s missing.
