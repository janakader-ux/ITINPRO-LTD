Why Do I Need an ITIN? 10 Common Reasons for UK-Based Applicants

Is an ITIN something only people living in America need, or could it be quietly blocking your property sale, pension payment or bank account here in the UK?

David is a British citizen who co-owns a U.S. LLC with friends, and his wife is American. He keeps hearing “just get an ITIN” but nobody tells him which of his situations actually requires one.

The reasons the IRS recognises

On Form W-7 you must tick the reason that best fits your case. In practice the common reasons are:

  • A non-resident alien who needs an ITIN to claim a tax treaty benefit (box a).
  • A non-resident alien filing a U.S. federal tax return, including only to claim a refund (box b).
  • A U.S. resident alien under the substantial presence test who files a return but cannot get an SSN (box c).
  • A dependent of a U.S. citizen or resident alien (box d) – only where claimed for an allowable tax benefit.
  • The spouse of a U.S. citizen or resident alien, for example on a joint return (box e).
  • A non-resident alien student, professor or researcher (box f).
  • A dependent or spouse of a non-resident alien holding a U.S. visa (box g).
  • “Other” (box h): the five exceptions, for example partnership or LLC interests, a U.S. bank account, pension, 401(k) or annuity payments, royalties, mortgage interest, or the sale of U.S. property.
  • Renewal of an ITIN that has expired.

Return required – or an exception

Every ITIN needs a federal tax purpose. Most applicants attach a valid U.S. return to Form W-7. Five exceptions let you apply without a return, if you can prove the exception with supporting documents (for example a letter from your bank or withholding agent).

The wrong box delays everything

Choosing the wrong reason box or sending the wrong supporting document is one of the most common causes of delay. That is exactly where an acceptance agent saves time.

Practical takeaway

  • Match your situation to one reason box on Form W-7.
  • No return? You need a documented exception.
  • Not sure which box? Ask before you post anything.
