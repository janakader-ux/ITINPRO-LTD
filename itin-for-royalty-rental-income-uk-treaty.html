ITIN for Royalties, Rental Income and Dividends: Claiming UK Treaty Benefits

U.S. tax is being deducted from your royalties at the full rate. Does the UK–U.S. tax treaty really let you pay less – and what has an ITIN to do with it?

An author in Norwich is paid U.S. royalties. The publisher says it can only apply a reduced treaty rate if it has a taxpayer number.

Why the ITIN is needed

A non-resident alien eligible for a reduced withholding rate under an income tax treaty needs a TIN to claim it (see Publication 515 on withholding, and Publication 901 on U.S. tax treaties).

How to fill the form

  • Tick box a (non-resident alien required to get an ITIN to claim tax treaty benefit) and box h.
  • Enter the exception on the h line, for example “Exception 1(d) – Royalty Income”.
  • Enter the treaty country and treaty article number under ‘Additional information’.
  • The treaty country must match the foreign address on line 3.

What to submit

An original letter or signed statement from the withholding agent on letterhead showing your name and confirming that an ITIN is needed to pay you this tax year. Self-generated statements are accepted only with the contract or a postmarked letter from the agent.

Practical takeaway

  • Treaty claim = box a + box h.
  • Treaty country and article number are mandatory.
  • The payer’s letter on letterhead is the key evidence.

A reader asks

“I don’t know which treaty article applies to me. Can you tell me?”

We will identify the article from the type of income you describe and show it on your form. We recommend a tax adviser confirm the treaty position for complex cases.
