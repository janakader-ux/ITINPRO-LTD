What Is an “Allowable Tax Benefit” for ITIN Purposes? HOH, QSS, AOTC, PTC, CDCC and ODC

You’ve listed your dependant on your return, but the IRS still rejected their ITIN. What did you miss?

Sofia’s tax software listed her niece as a dependant but didn’t tick the credit box because the niece had no ITIN yet.

The list

  • Joint return – spouse filing jointly.
  • Head of household (HOH) – tick the HOH filing status and list the applicant; they must have lived with you more than half the year or be your parent.
  • Qualifying surviving spouse (QSS) – your son, stepson, daughter or stepdaughter who lived with you all year.
  • American opportunity tax credit (AOTC) – attach Form 8863 listing the applicant.
  • Premium tax credit (PTC) – attach Form 8962.
  • Child and dependent care credit (CDCC) – attach Form 2441; allowed even if there is no tax to reduce.
  • Credit for other dependents (ODC) – the box next to the dependant’s name must be checked; dependant must be a U.S. resident or national.

The software trap

Listing a dependant is not an allowable benefit on its own. Tax software may not tick the ODC box for a dependant with no ID number. You must check it before sending the return with Form W-7.

HOH/QSS caution

An ITIN is not assigned or renewed for a HOH or QSS qualifying person who is not also claimed as a dependant on the attached return.

Practical takeaway

  • Seven benefits count; a dependant listing alone does not.
  • Check the ODC box yourself when using software.
  • Attach the form that matches the benefit (8863, 8962 or 2441).
