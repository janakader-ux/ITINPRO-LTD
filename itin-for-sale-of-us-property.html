ITIN for Selling a U.S. Property: FIRPTA Withholding and Exception 4

Your buyer’s lawyer says part of the sale price will be withheld for the IRS unless you produce an ITIN. Is that money gone?

George is selling a Texas condo he bought as an investment. Completion is in six weeks.

What the withholding is

When a U.S. real property interest is acquired from a foreign person, the buyer or transferee generally has a withholding obligation. This collects tax the seller may owe. In some cases the seller applies for a withholding certificate to reduce or eliminate withholding. An ITIN is needed for this transaction.

Exception 4 documents

  • A completed Form 8288, 8288-A or 8288-B.
  • A copy of the real estate sales contract or settlement statement.
  • As the seller, copies of the buyer’s Forms 8288 and 8288-A.
  • For a notice of non-recognition: documents showing the date, parties and type of transfer (for example a deed or like-kind exchange identification).

Form W-7

Tick box h and enter “Exception 4 – Disposition of Real Property” (FIRPTA). No tax return is required with the application.

Timing risk

Start early. The ITIN takes about seven weeks in a normal period, so apply as soon as the contract is signed.

Practical takeaway

  • Exception 4 = no return, contract plus Forms 8288 series.
  • Apply as soon as you exchange contracts.
  • Fees for property sales are priced per application.
