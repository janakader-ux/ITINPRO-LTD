ITIN for a Partnership Interest: Schedule K-1, 8805 and 1042-S Explained

Your partnership is about to send you a K-1 – but they can’t file it without your tax ID. Do you have to complete a full U.S. tax return just to get one?

Helen is a limited partner in a U.S. real-estate fund. The fund administrator told her: “No TIN, no distribution.”

Why partnerships ask

Information-reporting and withholding rules require partnerships and other third parties to request a TIN so they can file forms such as Form 8805 (Foreign Partner’s Information Statement of Section 1446 Withholding Tax), Form 1042-S and Schedule K-1.

Exception 1(a) in a sentence

Partners of a U.S. or foreign partnership that invests in the U.S. and who own assets that generate income subject to reporting or withholding can apply without attaching a return, provided the entity’s agreement is supplied.

Your checklist

  • Passport (original or certified).
  • Extract of the partnership or LLC agreement showing entity name, EIN, and your name and signature.
  • Form W-7 with box h ticked and “Exception 1(a) – A Partnership Interest”.
  • If treaty benefits are claimed: box a as well, and the treaty country and article number.

Timing

The withholding or reporting must take place in the current tax year, so apply early: the IRS aims to respond in about seven weeks (longer at peak times or from abroad).

Practical takeaway

  • K-1, 8805 and 1042-S reporting are what trigger the TIN request.
  • Exception 1(a): no return needed, agreement extract required.
  • Apply early – distributions can be held until the ITIN arrives.

A reader asks

“Which page of the partnership agreement do you need?”

The page showing the partnership name, its EIN and your name and signature, plus enough to show the partnership does business in the U.S. Send what you have and we’ll tell you what’s missing.
