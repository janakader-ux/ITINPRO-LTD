Form W-7 Line by Line: The Most Common Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

You’ve spent an evening filling in Form W-7 and the IRS sends it back. Which line tripped you up?

Ruth’s application was rejected because her foreign address was a PO box.

The traps

  • Line 1a: name exactly as on your documents; it must match your tax return. 1b is your birth name if different.
  • Line 2: a complete mailing address – the IRS uses it to return documents and send letters.
  • Line 3: your permanent foreign address. No PO boxes or ‘c/o’ addresses. If you no longer have a permanent foreign residence, enter just the country (but under reason b give the full foreign address).
  • Line 4: date of birth as MM/DD/YYYY, plus country of birth.
  • Line 6c/6d: visa class, number, expiry; document type, issuer, number, expiry and U.S. entry date. If you have never entered, write “Never entered the United States”.
  • Line 6e/6f: a previous ITIN or IRSN – required for renewals.
  • Line 6g: only if box f – college or company, city, and length of stay.

Check every line

If any field is blank or invalid, the application will be suspended or rejected. Enter N/A where something does not apply.

Made a mistake after submitting?

Do not file another Form W-7. Wait for the IRS to write to you and send the correct information with your reply.

Who signs

The applicant signs. For under-18s a parent or court-appointed guardian signs; a power of attorney must be on IRS Form 2848. If someone cannot sign their name they sign a mark in front of a witness, who also signs.

Practical takeaway

  • No PO boxes or ‘c/o’ on line 3.
  • Dates are MM/DD/YYYY; entry date must be complete.
  • Don’t re-submit a corrected form – reply to the IRS letter.

A reader asks

“Can I use the old version of Form W-7 I have saved?”

No. Use the current version; older versions can delay processing or be returned. We use the latest one.
